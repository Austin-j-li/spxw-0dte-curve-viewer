SPXW same-day options · 2 January to 30 September 2026

Curve gap test

Results, the reasons for the losses, and a straddle on the same rules · 7 October 2026

Neither curve made money with the gap rule, and neither curve did better than the other. The straight line lost 145 points a day and SVI lost 50 points a day in the main (extending) view. In the rolling one-week view, both were near zero.

The totals follow the index, not the curves. The index rose 11% in the period, so calls earned money and puts lost money with or without a signal.

A comparison inside each minute removes the index move. There, the options that each curve called cheap did as well as their neighbours, within a fraction of the bid-ask spread.

A straddle on the same rules shows the usual option premium. At 11 of 12 fixed times, it paid back 88 to 98 cents per dollar of mid price. Bought, it lost money; sold, it earned a little. No straddle result is clear of noise.

1The test

The question: with a fixed set of simple long strategies, which fair-value curve gives the better profit?

Rule
Each minute (at hh:mm:55), buy one contract of every out-of-the-money option in the chosen group whose curve price is above its ask by more than y points. Fill at the ask 5 seconds later. Hold to the official close.
Curves
Straight line: the line in strike between the mid implied volatilities of the nearest quoted neighbours below and above the strike (the strike itself is left out). SVI: the raw SVI fit of the same minute.
Settings
Group = put or call × near (|z| ≤ 0.5), mid (≤ 1.25) or far (≤ 2.5). Threshold y = 0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50 points. 30 settings and “no trade” for each curve.
Costs
The ask 5 seconds after the signal, plus a fee of 0.0119 to 0.0128 points per contract.
Walk-forward
Each week, take the setting with the best total in the fit window, and trade it in the next week. Extending: every day from 2 January to the end of the previous week. Rolling: the previous week only. 38 test weeks, 12 January to 30 September.
Days
135 days with built curves in 2026. 131 of them are test days.

3Why the runs lost

3.1The index direction sets the totals

The index rose 11%, from 6,887 at the first open to 7,652 at the last close. Bought with no signal at every minute, calls earned +0.67 (near), +0.63 (mid) and +0.01 (far) points a contract. Puts lost 1.40, 1.71 and 1.04.

From March (SVI) and April (straight line), the extending window chose mid calls and kept them. On those call days, the day profit moved with the index: the correlation with the day move is 0.46 for the line and 0.42 for SVI. The day profit varied by 900 to 1,650 points (sd a day).

3.2The signal does not pick cheaper options

Against the all-day reference, the line looks good on puts (+0.3 to +1.4 points a contract). That comparison mixes times of day and price levels. The same-minute comparison puts each bought option next to all options of its group bought in the same minute, so the index move and the clock are the same for both.

In that comparison, the line’s edge is −0.015 to +0.027 points a contract (all |t| below 1.6). The half-spread is 0.03 to 0.07 points. SVI goes from −0.29 (far puts, t −3.7) to +0.26 (near calls, t 1.6).

The gap against the reference comes from timing. Line signals come late in the day (30% to 46% after 14:00, against 15% to 17% of all options) and on cheaper options (near puts: mean ask 5.2 against 9.7). A cheaper option loses fewer points per contract.

3.3The straight line reads the bend of the smile

The smile is convex in strike. A straight line between two neighbour strikes therefore sits above the smile at the strike between them. The line implied volatility is above the option’s own mid implied volatility on 61% to 81% of option-minutes. So a positive line gap mostly measures the bend of the smile, not a cheap quote.

3.4Large SVI gaps mark weak fits

Weak SVI fits are 25% of the SVI option-minutes, but they give a gap above 0.05 points about 14 times as often as good fits. 79% of the SVI walk-forward contracts came from weak fits. A larger SVI gap lost more per contract.

4A straddle on the same rules

The second test keeps the days, the fills 5 seconds after the snapshot, the fees and the weekly windows. It replaces the curve signal with an at-the-money straddle: one call and one put at the listed strike nearest the forward.

Settings
One straddle a day at a fixed time (10:00 to 15:30, every 30 minutes), or one at each of the 12 times. 13 settings and “no trade”.
Menus
Buy both legs at the ask (the long rule of the first test). Sell both legs at the bid (the mirror). Each menu has its own walk-forward.
Payout
|close − strike| at the official close. All 1,620 entries (135 days × 12 times) filled.

The straddle paid back 88 to 98 cents per dollar of mid price at 11 of the 12 times; 15:00 paid back 104 cents. So the buy menu lost money. The rolling window lost the most (−11.2 points a day, t −2.4), because it moved each week to the time that did best in the previous week.

The sell menu earned a little: +7.9 points a day (extending) and +4.5 (rolling), both with t below 1. A sale at all 12 times made +0.83 points per straddle, but one day (9 March, index +1.6%) took 727 points. Earlier work on June 2022 to December 2023 found the same sign: the straddle paid back 88% to 97% of its price after 10:30.

5Conclusion

Long options held to the close measure the path of the index more than the quality of a curve. On these 135 days, neither curve found options that were cheap by more than a fraction of the spread. The same-minute comparison is the direct test of which curve prices better, and there the two curves are equal within noise.

The straddle shows the same market from the other side. Option buyers pay a small premium on average, and 131 days are too few to show the seller’s gain clearly.

A cleaner next test of the curves would ask whether the price of a “cheap” option moves toward the curve in the next few minutes. That answer does not depend on where the index closes.

6Limits

  • All 135 days were used in earlier work. This test describes the data; it does not confirm a result.
  • No result is adjusted for multiple tests: 60 curve cells, 26 straddle cells and 8 walk-forward series.
  • Straddles at fixed times in 2026 were looked at in earlier work, before this test.
  • Nothing caps the loss of a straddle sale on a large-move day.
  • From January to June, the data holds about 3 days a week (sealed SVI reserve days are left out).
  • Sections 3.2 to 3.4 were measured after the curve run, on the same trades.
Data: release v1 of the SPXW same-day curve file (one curve set a minute at hh:mm:55) and the raw 5-second quotes; settlement at the FRED SP500 official close.
